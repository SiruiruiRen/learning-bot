# Proposed Pre/Post Knowledge Check Questions

Based on the original Qualtrics intervention design, this document outlines the exact PRE-test and POST-test questions used for adaptive learning and knowledge assessment.

## Overview

**Adaptive Logic**: For each topic, students take a PRE-test with 2 questions (1 definition + 1 scenario). If they score **2/2 correct**, the video is skipped and they see a 1-sentence summary. If they score **<2/2**, they watch the full video. After the video (or summary), all students complete POST-test questions to check understanding.

---

## Module 1.1: Self-testing

### PRE-TEST (Before Video - Adaptive Skip Logic)

**Q1 (Definition - Multiple Select):**
> Self-testing is a useful learning strategy for what reason?
> - [ ] It helps emphasize retrieval practice and bring back information you already know ✅
> - [ ] It helps students remember key information for a longer period of time ✅
> - [ ] It helps with future learning ✅
> 
> *All three must be selected for correct*

**Q2 (Scenario - Multiple Choice):**
> You just began a unit of a course and have 4 weeks of topics to cover before the unit exam. For each new lesson, the guided reading questions and course outlines make clear that there are a number of facts, definitions, and properties that you'll need to be able to recall when the exam comes.
> 
> Pick the best strategy, from those below, that will enable you to rehearse this knowledge and ensure you can recall it.
> 
> - [ ] Block out the entire day before the exam. Redo all your homework questions to be sure you've rehearsed fully. ❌
> - [ ] Block out the entire day before the exam. Reread all the chapters to be sure you're on top of all the material. ❌
> - [ ] After reading each chapter in advance of the day it is covered, reread each chapter repeatedly in advance of the exam. ❌
> - [ ] After turning in a homework assignment by the deadline, set up reminders in your calendar to re-complete that homework again at least a few times before the exam. ✅

**Skip Rule**: 2/2 correct → Skip video, show summary. <2/2 → Show video.

---

### POST-TEST (After Video/Summary - All Students)

**Q1 (Multiple Choice):**
> Which learning strategy does research suggest is more effective in improving later performance?
> - [ ] Rereading learning materials ❌
> - [ ] Rehearsing the information by repeatedly testing whether you can recall it ✅

**Q2 (Open-ended - List):**
> List the 3 ways that self-testing helps you learn.
> 1. ________________
> 2. ________________
> 3. ________________
> 
> **Sample Answers:**
> 1. It helps you to remember the information longer.
> 2. It helps you find gaps in your knowledge.
> 3. It improves your future studying.

**Self-Assessment:**
> Did your answers match the sample answers?
> - [ ] Yes
> - [ ] Somewhat
> - [ ] No
> 
> *If No/Somewhat: "In a few sentences, note the differences between your answers and the sample answers. Then explain one way you can use self-testing to help you study this semester."*

## Module 1.2: Spacing

### PRE-TEST (Before Video - Adaptive Skip Logic)

**Q1 (Definition - Multiple Choice):**
> What does the spacing effect show?
> - [ ] Spacing is only useful for memorization tasks ❌
> - [ ] Spacing your practice over time leads to better long-term retention than massing practice ✅
> - [ ] Spacing requires more total study time ❌

**Q2 (Scenario - Multiple Choice):**
> Ana, Brad, Cora, and Deneshia all have 6 hours to study for an exam. They each use a different strategy:
> - Ana: Studies 1 hour per day for 6 days
> - Brad: Studies 3 hours on day 1, 3 hours on day 2
> - Cora: Studies all 6 hours the day before the exam
> - Deneshia: Studies 2 hours on day 1, 2 hours on day 3, 2 hours on day 5
> 
> Based on spacing research, who will likely perform best on the exam one week later?
> - [ ] Ana ✅ (best spacing)
> - [ ] Brad
> - [ ] Cora ❌ (massing)
> - [ ] Deneshia

**Skip Rule**: 2/2 correct → Skip video, show summary. <2/2 → Show video.

---

### POST-TEST (After Video/Summary - All Students)

**Q1 (Multiple Choice):**
> Assuming you spend an equal amount of study time ON each strategy, which of the following does research suggest is more effective for learning?
> - [ ] Distributing the studying and restudying of the same content over a long period of time ✅
> - [ ] Massing the studying and restudying of the same content over a short period of time ❌

**Q2 (Open-ended):**
> Describe how you can combine the self-testing and spacing strategies.
> 
> **Sample Answer:**
> "You can combine the two strategies by spacing your self-testing on given content. For example, you could self-test after reading a chapter and then decide to self-test on that chapter again after two weeks."

**Self-Assessment:**
> Does your reasoning match the reasoning in the sample answer?
> - [ ] Yes
> - [ ] Somewhat
> - [ ] No
> 
> *If No/Somewhat: "In a few sentences, highlight the key pieces of the model answer that you also included in yours. Then point out the pieces of the model answer you missed, and elaborate on what you would add to or change about your answer."*

## Module 1.3: Self-explanation

### PRE-TEST (Before Video - Adaptive Skip Logic)

**Q1 (Definition - Multiple Choice):**
> What is self-explanation?
> - [ ] Explaining concepts to a study partner ❌
> - [ ] Actively generating explanations for yourself about how and why things work ✅
> - [ ] Reading explanations in the textbook ❌

**Q2 (Scenario - Multiple Choice):**
> You're studying cellular respiration. Which approach best demonstrates self-explanation?
> - [ ] Reading the textbook chapter on cellular respiration three times ❌
> - [ ] Creating flashcards with definitions of key terms ❌
> - [ ] After reading about ATP production, pausing to explain in your own words: "So ATP is produced when... and this matters because..." ✅
> - [ ] Highlighting all important sentences in the chapter ❌

**Skip Rule**: 2/2 correct → Skip video, show summary. <2/2 → Show video.

---

### POST-TEST (After Video/Summary - All Students)

**Q1 (Practice Exercise - Open-ended):**
> Practice self-explanation:
> 
> **Example 1: How does blood move through the circulatory system?**
> 
> [Text passage provided about circulatory system]
> 
> In the space below, explain how the circulatory system works in as much detail as you can recall.
> 
> **Your Answer:** ________________
> 
> **Sample Answer:** [Detailed explanation provided]
> 
> **Self-Assessment:**
> How close was your answer to the sample explanation? In the space below, note:
> 1. How well were you able to explain how the circulatory system works?
> 2. In what areas or ways did you have difficulty explaining the circulatory system?
> 3. What could you do to improve your learning if this were a learning objective in your course?

**Q2 (Reflection - Open-ended):**
> Explain how self-testing your response to how the circulatory system works improved your understanding of the concept?
> 
> **Sample Answer:**
> "When I self-test, I practice retrieving information and organizing it in different ways. This creates new neural connections that make it easier to retrieve the information later, and even learn more after I self-test. Moreover, since I also attempt to generate an answer during self-testing (instead of just relying on the broad feeling of knowing based on my familiarity with the topic), I am more likely to identify gaps in my understanding."

## Module 2.1: Self-Regulated Learning (SRL) - Define Task

### PRE-TEST (Before Video - Adaptive Skip Logic)

**Q1 (Definition - Multiple Choice):**
> What is self-regulated learning?
> - [ ] It is when students regulate their peer's learning and monitor their performance to give them tips of improvement ❌
> - [ ] It is when students take charge of their learning and monitor their progress throughout the learning process ✅
> - [ ] It is when students ask their teacher how they learn best and copy that learning strategy ❌
> - [ ] It is when students plan how to learn a lot of information two days before a test ❌

**Q2 (Scenario - Multiple Choice):**
> The first stage in self-regulated learning is defining a task. Which student effectively completes the first stage?
> - [ ] Alice consults the course schedule and assigns blocks on her own calendar to complete all the assigned readings. During those times, she pays close attention to the key vocabulary words highlighted in the text. ❌
> - [ ] Barry develops a system that he applies when he completes each assigned reading: he color codes all his material while studying and assigns a color to each concept definition ❌
> - [ ] Cora seeks out the syllabus to review the course objectives her instructor lists, and the assigned content for the upcoming lessons in the unit. She then reads the learning objectives that precedes the textbook sections she is to read, and commits them to memory. ❌
> - [ ] Dana considers what she will need to learn in order to succeed in the course and begins by consulting the learning objectives in the syllabus and those listed at the beginning of each assigned lesson or reading. She first considers the topic to be covered, then considers the level of understanding she will need to demonstrate, when tested on it on course exams. ✅

**Skip Rule**: 2/2 correct → Skip video, show summary. <2/2 → Show video.

---

### POST-TEST (After Video/Summary - All Students)

**Q1 (Fill-in-the-blank):**
> List all stages of the four-stage model of self-regulated learning.
> 
> Stage 1: ________________
> Stage 2: ________________
> Stage 3: ________________
> Stage 4: ________________
> 
> **Sample Answers:**
> - Stage 1: Define the task
> - Stage 2: Set goals and develop a plan
> - Stage 3: Execute the plan
> - Stage 4: Monitor your learning (and adapt if needed)

**Q2 (Application - Open-ended):**
> Consider the following learning objective:
> "Recite the limit definition of derivative."
> 
> What level of understanding does this learning objective suggest you would need to achieve?
> 
> **Your Answer:** ________________
> 
> **Sample Answer:** The verb "recite" suggests that this learning objective requires a knowledge level of understanding.
> 
> Name a strategy (or strategies) that you could use to develop the level of understanding you determined for the above-mentioned learning objective.
> 
> **Your Answer:** ________________
> 
> **Sample Answer:** A simple rehearsal should suffice for a learning objective at the knowledge level of understanding. One could create a flashcard for the definition and practice retrieving it. Memorization is easier if what is being learned is meaningful; therefore, trying to imagine what the definition represents could be one way to make it more likely that you will remember it longer.

## Module 3.1: Mental Contrasting

### PRE-TEST (Before Video - Adaptive Skip Logic)

**Q1 (Definition - Multiple Choice):**
> What does mental contrasting help you do?
> - [ ] Only focus on positive outcomes ❌
> - [ ] Visualize your goal and identify obstacles, then create plans to overcome them ✅
> - [ ] Avoid thinking about obstacles ❌

**Q2 (Scenario - Multiple Choice):**
> Addy, Blair, Carrie, and Darryl all want to walk 10,000 steps daily. Who is using mental contrasting?
> - [ ] Addy: "I'll walk 10,000 steps every day!" (only positive) ❌
> - [ ] Blair: "I might be too busy, so I'll never do this" (only obstacles) ❌
> - [ ] Carrie: "I want to walk 10,000 steps daily. I might be too busy on weekdays, so I'll schedule walks during lunch breaks and use a standing desk." ✅
> - [ ] Darryl: "I'll try to walk more" (vague) ❌

**Skip Rule**: 2/2 correct → Skip video, show summary. <2/2 → Show video.

---

### POST-TEST (After Video/Summary - All Students)

**Q1 (Fill-in-the-blank):**
> Describe the 3 steps of mental contrasting.
>

## Implementation Recommendations

### For Current Chatbot Design

1. **Replace current knowledge check questions** with these stronger scenario-based questions from the original design.

2. **Consider adaptive skipping**:
   - Option A: Full adaptive (PRE-test → skip video if 2/2 → POST-test)
   - Option B: Simplified (one quick PRE question → optional skip → POST questions)
   - Option C: Keep fixed path, just use better POST questions

3. **Question format**:
   - PRE: Multiple choice (definition + scenario) for quick assessment
   - POST: Mix of MC, open-ended, and self-assessment for deeper learning

4. **Self-assessment workflow**: After POST questions, show sample answers and ask students to compare their responses. This metacognitive reflection is a key learning component.

5. **Scenario questions are pedagogically stronger** than pure definition questions because they test application, not just recall.

---

## Notes

- The original design uses **2/2 correct** as the threshold for skipping videos. This is strict but ensures students truly understand before skipping.
- POST questions always include **open-ended reflection** components to promote deeper processing.
- **Self-assessment** (comparing to sample answers) is a critical metacognitive component that should be preserved.
- Scenario questions use **realistic student names and situations** to increase engagement and relevance.
